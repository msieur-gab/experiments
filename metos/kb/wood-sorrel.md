title: Wood sorrel
aka: common wood sorrel, cuckoo bread, shamrock, Waldsauerklee, Sauerklee, oxalis petite oseille, pain de coucou, Oxalis acetosella

warn: It contains oxalic acid. Eat only small amounts, and avoid it if you have kidney stones, kidney problems or gout.

A small woodland plant with clover-like leaves and a sharp, lemony taste. It is a refreshing nibble and a sour seasoning, not a main food. It grows in shady, damp woods, often under beech and spruce.

## identify
- Three heart-shaped leaflets on each leaf, each with a notch at the tip, joined at the point.
- Leaflets fold down along their middle at night, in rain or in strong sun, like a closed umbrella.
- Each leaf grows on its own thin, often reddish stalk straight from the ground. There is no leafy stem.
- Clear sour, lemony taste when chewed.
- Single small white flowers with fine purple-pink veins, on their own stalks, in April and May.
- Low, 5 to 15 cm, often forming carpets.
- Grows in shady, damp woods, on mossy ground and old tree stumps.

## lookalikes
Clovers (Trifolium) also have three leaflets, but theirs are rounded or oval, without the heart shape, and they do not taste sour. Clovers are not dangerous.

Yellow-flowered sorrels (other Oxalis species) grow in gardens and between paving stones. They are similar and also contain oxalic acid.

There are no dangerous lookalikes in Central Europe if you check for three heart-shaped leaflets that fold and a sour taste.

## eat
- Leaves, flowers and young seed pods can be eaten raw.
- Use a few leaves in salads, on bread, in soups at the end, or in sauces for fish, like lemon.
- Keep it to a small handful at a time. Do not eat it every day in quantity.
- Do not give large amounts to children.

## harvest
- Leaves from April to October; most tender in spring.
- Pinch off single leaves and leave the plant in place. Carpets regrow slowly.
- In parts of Central Europe, fox tapeworm eggs are a concern on low-growing plants in woods. Wash the leaves well. Cooking kills the eggs.
- Do not pick next to forest roads or where dogs are walked.
