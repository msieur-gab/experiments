title: Purslane
aka: common purslane, pigweed, little hogweed, Portulak, Gemüseportulak, Burzelkraut, pourpier, pourpier potager, Portulaca oleracea
tags: plant wild food
warn: Break a stem: purslane has clear, watery sap. Spurges grow in the same places, have white milky sap and are toxic.
see: deadly-lookalikes, foraging-rules

A low, juicy, succulent weed that spreads flat over bare, warm soil. Leaves and stems are eaten raw or cooked, with a fresh, slightly sour and salty taste. It grows in gardens, vegetable beds, vineyards, between paving stones and on bare ground in warm summers.

## identify
- Sap clear and watery when a stem is broken, never milky.
- Stems thick, round, smooth and juicy, often reddish, spreading flat from the centre like spokes.
- Leaves thick and fleshy, smooth and shiny, shaped like a small paddle or spoon, rounded at the tip, often in clusters at the stem ends.
- Flowers tiny, yellow, five petals, open only in sunny mornings.
- Small seed capsules that open with a lid, full of tiny black seeds.
- Grows low, rarely more than 10 cm high, on open, sunny, disturbed soil.
- Appears June to September, after the soil warms up.

## lookalikes
- Spurges (Wolfsmilch, euphorbe), such as petty spurge and spotted spurge, are toxic. They grow in the same gardens and pavement cracks. Their stems are thin and wiry, the leaves thin and not fleshy, and broken stems release white milky sap that irritates skin and eyes. If you see milky sap, do not eat it.
- Stonecrops (Mauerpfeffer, orpin), such as biting stonecrop, grow on walls, rocks and dry sand. Their leaves are small, thick and pointed or bead-like, packed along upright shoots, and the yellow flowers are star-shaped. Biting stonecrop tastes sharp and peppery and can make you ill; do not eat it.
- Mature plants are easy to tell by the clear sap and flat, paddle-shaped, glossy leaves on reddish, juicy stems.

## eat
- Leaves and tender stem tips raw in salad, or in sandwiches.
- Cooked briefly in soups and stews, where it thickens slightly; or fried with eggs.
- Thicker stems can be pickled in vinegar.
- It contains oxalic acid, like spinach. A side portion is fine for most people; people with kidney stones should eat little.

## harvest
- June to September. Pinch or cut the tender tips and leave the base: it regrows.
- Best before the flowers and seed capsules form; older stems get tough.
- Wash well; it grows flat on soil and collects grit.
- Avoid pavements, roadsides, parking areas and sprayed vineyards or fields.
