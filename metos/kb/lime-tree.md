A large tree with heart-shaped leaves, planted in villages, avenues and parks and also wild in mixed woodland. Its young leaves are a mild salad green in spring, and its flowers make a well-known calming tea. Shoots growing from the base of the trunk put fresh leaves within easy reach.

## identify
- Flowers in summer: small, creamy-yellow, strongly sweet-scented, in hanging clusters attached to a long, pale green, tongue-shaped leafy wing (bract). Bees hum in the tree.
- Fruit: small round hard nutlets, still hanging from the same pale wing into autumn.
- Leaves heart-shaped with a pointed tip, finely toothed, and lopsided at the base (one side lower than the other). They alternate along the twig.
- Small-leaved lime has tufts of rusty-brown hairs where the veins meet on the leaf underside. Large-leaved lime has bigger, softer, hairy leaves.
- Many trees have a dense cluster of shoots around the base of the trunk.
- Bark grey, smooth on young trees, later with shallow ridges.
- Flowers June to July.

## lookalikes
- Hazel leaves are rounder, double-toothed and roughly hairy, and hazel is a many-stemmed shrub with nuts. See hazel.
- Lilac has heart-shaped leaves too, but they are smooth-edged with no teeth and grow in opposite pairs.
- Indian bean tree (catalpa), planted in parks, has very large heart-shaped leaves with no teeth, often in groups of three, and long thin bean pods. Do not eat it.
- Birch leaves are smaller and triangular, on a tree with white bark.
- The key checks for lime: toothed edge, lopsided base, alternate leaves, and the pale winged bract with flowers or nutlets.

## eat
- Young leaves raw in salad or as a sandwich green. They are mild and slightly slimy in a pleasant way. They become tough in early summer.
- Flowers, picked with their pale bract, dried in the shade, make tea: a spoonful steeped in hot water for several minutes. It is traditionally used to calm and to help sleep. Drink in moderation.
- Fresh flowers can be eaten in small amounts.
- Leaves can be eaten in meal-sized amounts as a salad green.

## harvest
- Leaves: April to May, while bright green, thin and soft. Basal shoots and low branches keep producing soft leaves a little longer.
- Flowers: June to July, when most flowers on a cluster have just opened. Dry them quickly in an airy, shaded place and store dry.
- Avoid trees along busy roads and car parks. Aphids often cover the leaves in sticky honeydew in summer; wash or choose clean leaves.
