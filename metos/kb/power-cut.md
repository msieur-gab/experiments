Most power cuts end within a few hours, but a large one can last days. Stay informed, keep warm, keep food cold, and stay safe from fire and fumes.

## how
1. Light: use battery torches and lanterns. If you must use candles, keep them away from anything that burns, never leave them alone, and air the room regularly.
2. News: listen to a battery, solar or crank radio, or a car radio, for official announcements.
3. Phone: save the battery. Charge it from a power bank or in the car. Keep important numbers written on paper.
4. Food: keep the fridge and freezer closed (see power-cut-food). Eat what spoils first and food that needs no cooking.
5. Cooking: small meals on a camping stove or grill, outside only.
6. Warmth: warm clothes and blankets, everyone in one room with the door closed (see heat-one-room).
7. Water: use bottled, boiled or treated water if the supply is affected (see water-storage, water-boil).
8. Medicines: refrigerated medicines that have been without cooling for a day or more should be replaced, unless the label says otherwise. If a life depends on one, keep using it until you get a new supply.
9. Money: card payment and cash machines stop. Use the cash you keep at home.
10. Neighbours: check on older, sick or disabled neighbours and families with small children.

## safety
Never heat the home with a gas cooker or oven. Never use a charcoal grill, camping stove or generator inside (see generator-safety). Keep a fire extinguisher at hand. Never touch a fallen power line. If it gets too cold or too hot at home, go to friends or a public warming place. If someone may be dangerously cold (hypothermia), get medical help at once: call 112.
