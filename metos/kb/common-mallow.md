Common mallow and its smaller relative dwarf mallow grow on paths, field edges and waste ground. Leaves, flowers and the small green seed heads are all edible, with a mild taste. Cooked leaves become slightly slimy, which thickens soups.

## identify
- Flowers have five petals, each notched at the tip. Common mallow: pink-purple petals with darker stripes, 2.5 to 4 cm wide. Dwarf mallow: smaller, pale pink to almost white.
- Leaves are round to kidney-shaped, with five to seven shallow lobes and rounded teeth, on long stalks.
- Veins spread out from the base of the leaf like the fingers of a hand.
- Leaves are soft and slightly hairy, not glossy.
- Green seed heads are flat, round discs made of segments, like a tiny wheel of cheese.
- No strong smell. Chewed leaves feel slimy.
- Common mallow grows upright or sprawling, 30 to 120 cm. Dwarf mallow creeps along the ground.
- Grows in sunny, dry places: paths, walls, field edges, waste ground, near villages and farms.

## lookalikes
Marsh marigold (Caltha palustris) is poisonous. Its leaves are round or kidney-shaped but glossy and hairless, and it has large yellow flowers. It grows in wet places: streams, ditches and marshy meadows, where mallow does not.

Lesser celandine (Ficaria verna) is poisonous raw. Its leaves are glossy, hairless and heart-shaped, and it has yellow star-shaped flowers in early spring.

Cranesbills (Geranium) have rounded leaves too, but cut much more deeply, and often a strong smell. They are not dangerous.

Ground ivy (Glechoma) has small rounded leaves on a square stem and smells strongly when crushed. It is not dangerous.

If the leaves are glossy and hairless or the flowers are yellow, it is not mallow.

## eat
- Young leaves raw in salads, or cooked in soups and stews, where they act as a thickener.
- Flowers raw, in salads.
- Green seed heads ("cheeses") raw as a nibble, or cooked. They taste mild and nutty.
- Older leaves are better cooked.
- Like spinach, mallow can hold more nitrate when it grows on heavily manured ground. Eat normal vegetable portions.

## harvest
- Young leaves from April to September; flowers from June to September; seed heads while still green.
- Pick leaves and flowers by hand and leave the plant to regrow.
- It often grows next to paths and walls: avoid roadsides, dog-walking paths, manure heaps and sprayed field edges.
