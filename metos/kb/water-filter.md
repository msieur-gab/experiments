Filtering removes mud, sediment and some taste. It is a first step that makes disinfection work better, not a replacement for it.

## how
Settling:
1. Fill a container and leave it still for several hours.
2. Carefully pour or scoop the clear water from the top, leaving the sediment.

Cloth filter:
Pour the water through several layers of clean, tightly woven cloth.

Layered filter (a cut bottle, bucket or sock):
1. Make a small hole at the bottom and cover it with cloth.
2. Add layers from bottom to top: fine gravel, crushed charcoal from a wood fire (not ash, not briquettes), fine sand, then coarse sand or gravel on top.
3. Pour water in slowly at the top and collect it below.
4. Throw away the first water that comes through.
5. Disinfect the result.

```
      dirty water in
           |
    |~~~~~~v~~~~~~|
    |  coarse sand |  <- or gravel, catches big dirt
    |..............|
    |  fine sand   |
    |..............|
    |  charcoal    |  <- crushed, from a wood fire
    |..............|
    |  fine gravel |
    |__cloth_______|
           |
           v
     filtered water  -> still disinfect it
```

## safety
Most portable filters do not remove viruses. Charcoal from a fire is not the same as activated carbon and removes much less. Chemicals and fuel pass through improvised filters.
