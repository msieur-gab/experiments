title: Ribwort plantain
aka: ribwort, narrowleaf plantain, English plantain, lamb's tongue, Spitzwegerich, plantain lancéolé, Plantago lanceolata

warn: Before it flowers, a tuft of narrow leaves can be confused with young bulb leaves such as daffodil, which are poisonous. Check for the strong parallel ribs with stringy fibres inside.

A very common plant of meadows, lawns and paths, with narrow ribbed leaves and small brown flower heads on long stalks. The young leaves and flower buds are edible. Crushed leaves are also a well-known folk remedy for insect stings and nettle stings.

## identify
- Narrow, lance-shaped leaves, 10 to 30 cm long, all growing from the ground in a rosette.
- Three to seven strong ribs run along the length of each leaf, parallel to each other.
- Tear a leaf across: stringy fibres pull out of the ribs.
- Flowers are small, dense, oval brown heads on top of long, leafless, grooved stalks, taller than the leaves.
- When flowering, the heads carry a ring of white stamens, like a little crown.
- Leaves are slightly hairy or smooth, and have no smell.
- Grows in meadows, lawns, paths, field edges and waste ground, often in trampled places.
- Leaves from spring to autumn; flowers from April to September.

## lookalikes
Young leaves of bulb plants such as daffodils grow in tufts and are poisonous. They are smooth, fleshy and strap-shaped, grow from a bulb and have no stringy ribs. Ribwort grows from a short root, not a bulb.

Broadleaf plantain (Plantago major) has broad, oval leaves with the same ribs and fibres. It is also edible.

Grasses have narrow leaves too, but their leaves grow on stems with joints, not in a flat rosette.

Once it has its brown flower heads on long stalks, ribwort is hard to confuse with anything dangerous.

## eat
- Young leaves from the centre of the rosette can be eaten raw, chopped finely across the ribs, because whole leaves are fibrous.
- Older leaves are better cooked in soups or stews.
- Young flower buds, picked before the white stamens show, can be eaten raw or fried. Many people find they taste a little like mushrooms.
- Seeds can be added to bread or porridge in small amounts.
- Eat it as part of a meal. It is not a main green in large amounts.

## harvest
- Young leaves from April to June are the most tender. New leaves grow again after mowing.
- Flower buds from May to August.
- Pick leaf by leaf with fingers or scissors and leave the root.
- Do not pick on paths used by dogs, in parks, on roadsides or near sprayed fields. It often grows in exactly these places.
