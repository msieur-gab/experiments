title: Stinging nettle
aka: nettle, nettles, urtica, urtica dioica, ortie
tags: plant wild food fibre
warn: Wear gloves to pick. Do not eat raw unless crushed or dried. Never pick from roadsides or sprayed fields.
see: foraging-rules
source: compiled from general foraging references, verify before relying on it

A common, very nutritious wild green that grows near people, paths and rich soil. One of the safest plants to learn first because its sting makes it hard to confuse.

## identify
Upright stems up to about 1.5 m, square in section. Leaves in opposite pairs, heart-shaped to pointed, with toothed edges. The whole plant is covered in fine stinging hairs. Small greenish flowers hang in strings in summer.

## harvest
Pick the top four to six young leaves in spring, before the plant flowers. Older plants are tough and less pleasant. Cutting plants back makes them regrow fresh tops later in the season.

## eat
Cooking, blanching or drying removes the sting completely. Use like spinach: soup, stew, with eggs or potatoes. Dried leaves make tea.

## use
Stem fibres can be made into string and cord. Nettles make a strong liquid fertiliser when soaked in water for two to three weeks.
