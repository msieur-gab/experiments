title: Red clover
aka: clover, meadow clover, purple clover, Rotklee, Wiesenklee, trèfle des prés, trèfle violet, trèfle rouge, Trifolium pratense
tags: plant wild food
warn: Use only fresh or well-dried flowers, never mouldy ones. Avoid larger amounts in pregnancy, while breastfeeding, or when taking blood thinners.

A common meadow plant with round pink-purple flower heads. The flowers are sweet, edible raw and make tea, and the plant is easy to recognise. It grows in meadows, pastures, lawns and along paths almost everywhere in Central Europe.

## identify
- Flower heads round, about 2 to 3 cm, pink to purple, made of many small tube-shaped flowers. Usually a pair of leaves sits directly under the head.
- Leaves in threes (trifoliate); each leaflet oval, often with a pale V or crescent mark across the middle.
- Stems and leaves slightly hairy.
- Low to medium height, about 15 to 50 cm, often in patches.
- Flowers May to September.

## lookalikes
- White clover is smaller, creeps along the ground and has white heads on long separate stalks. Its flowers are also edible.
- Other clovers and lucerne (alfalfa) have similar leaves. Lucerne has purple flowers in longer clusters and leaflets toothed near the tip. Lucerne sprouts and seeds are best not eaten in quantity.
- Wood sorrel has three heart-shaped leaflets that fold down, and single white flowers. It is sour from oxalic acid; eat only small amounts.
- Yellow-flowered plants with three leaflets, such as black medick or hop trefoil, are not red clover.
- Crown vetch and vetches have pink flowers but leaves made of many small leaflets in rows, not three. Do not eat them.
- No deadly plant in Central Europe looks closely like red clover in flower.

## eat
- Flowers raw: pull out the small tubes and suck the sweet base, or scatter them on salad.
- Tea: steep a few fresh or dried flower heads in hot water for several minutes.
- Young leaves: cook them briefly. Raw leaves are hard to digest and can cause bloating; eat only small amounts.
- Flowers can be dried and ground into flour mixtures in small amounts.
- Red clover contains plant compounds that act like weak hormones (isoflavones). A few flower heads or cups of tea are generally considered fine, but avoid making it a large part of the diet.

## harvest
- Flowers: May to September, when fully pink and fresh. Skip heads that are turning brown.
- Dry flowers quickly in a shaded, airy place. Throw them away if they smell musty or show mould: mouldy clover can form a substance that stops blood from clotting.
- Avoid lawns and verges that may be sprayed, fields grazed by animals, and dog-walking areas.
