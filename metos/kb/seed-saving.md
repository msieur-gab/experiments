Saving seed means you do not depend on buying it each year. Start with plants that pollinate themselves.

## how
Easiest: beans, peas, tomatoes and lettuce. They mostly pollinate themselves, so seeds grow like the parent.

Harder: squash, courgette, cucumber, cabbage family and maize. They cross with other varieties nearby, so they need distance or covering to stay true.

1. Choose your healthiest, best plants as seed parents.
2. Beans and peas: let pods dry on the plant, then shell.
3. Tomatoes: scoop the seeds with their gel into a jar with a little water, leave 2 to 4 days until a mould layer forms, rinse well, and dry on a plate.
4. Lettuce: let a plant flower and go to seed, then shake the dry heads into a bag.
5. Dry all seeds fully before storing.

## store
Store in paper envelopes or jars, cool, dry and dark. Label every packet with the plant, variety and year. Most seeds keep two to five years.
