title: Ground elder
aka: goutweed, bishop's weed, herb gerard, Giersch, égopode podagraire, herbe aux goutteux, Aegopodium podagraria
tags: plant wild food
warn: Ground elder belongs to the carrot family, which includes deadly hemlocks. Only eat it if every feature matches, especially the leaf divided into three, then three again. If you are not sure, leave it.
see: deadly-lookalikes, foraging-rules

A spreading plant that forms dense carpets in gardens, hedges and woodland edges. Gardeners fight it as a weed, but the young leaves are a good green, raw or cooked. It is worth knowing because where it grows it is plentiful, but it needs care because of its poisonous relatives.

## identify
- Leaf stalk divides into three, and each part carries three leaflets: often nine leaflets on the lower leaves. Upper leaves may have only three.
- The leaf stalk is three-edged, with a groove on its upper side.
- Leaflets are broad, oval and pointed, 4 to 8 cm long, with toothed edges, soft and hairless. They are often a little lopsided.
- Crushed leaves smell of carrot or parsley. They never smell unpleasant or mousy.
- Grows in dense patches from creeping white underground stems, so you find many plants together, not one alone.
- Young leaves in spring are pale, shiny and folded.
- Flat white umbrella-shaped flower heads on stems up to about 1 m, from May to July.
- Likes shade and half-shade: gardens, hedges, parks, woodland edges.

## lookalikes
Hemlock (Conium maculatum) has fine, fern-like leaves cut into many small parts, a smooth stem with purple blotches, and a mousy smell. Ground elder has broad, plain leaflets and a green stem.

Fool's parsley (Aethusa cynapium) is poisonous and grows in gardens. Its leaves are finely divided and glossy, and it smells unpleasant. Ground elder's leaflets are never finely cut.

Water hemlock (Cicuta virosa) grows in wet places and has narrow, finely divided leaflets. Ground elder rarely grows in water or swamps.

Young elder (Sambucus) has leaves with five to seven leaflets in pairs, on a woody stem. Raw elder leaves cause nausea. Ground elder has no woody stem.

Dog's mercury (Mercurialis perennis) is poisonous and grows in the same shady woods. Its leaves are single and undivided, in opposite pairs on an upright stem.

Never identify ground elder by its flowers. Many carrot-family plants have white umbrella flowers. Use the leaf pattern, the leaf stalk, the smell and the way it grows in carpets.

## eat
- Young leaves, before flowering, are best. Eat them raw in salads or cook them like spinach.
- The taste is like a mix of parsley and carrot.
- Young leaf stalks can be cooked as a vegetable.
- Older leaves after flowering taste stronger and are tougher. Use them cooked and in moderation.
- Start with a small amount, as with any new plant.

## harvest
- March to May for the young, shiny leaves. Cutting a patch back in summer gives fresh leaves again.
- Pick leaf by leaf. Check each leaf, because other plants can grow inside the patch.
- Do not pick near roadsides, sprayed hedges or dog-walking paths.
