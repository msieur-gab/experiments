The wild strawberry is a small, low plant with tiny, very aromatic red fruit. It grows on woodland edges, clearings, banks and path sides across Central Europe, and its leaves can also be used for tea.

## identify
- Low plant, rarely more than 20 cm, spreading with long thin runners that root.
- Leaves of three toothed leaflets, bright green and slightly hairy; the end tooth of each leaflet is as long as or longer than the teeth beside it.
- White flowers with five round petals and a yellow centre, from spring.
- Small red fruit, about 1 to 2 cm, shaped like a garden strawberry, with tiny seeds on the outside, usually hanging down.
- Strong sweet strawberry smell and taste.
- Grows in light woods, clearings, banks, path edges and sunny slopes.

## lookalikes
Mock strawberry (yellow-flowered strawberry) has yellow flowers and red fruit that stands upright and tastes of almost nothing. It is not considered dangerous. Barren strawberry has white flowers but no juicy fruit; its leaflets have a short end tooth, shorter than the teeth beside it.

The real risk is other red berries growing low in the woods: lily of the valley (red berries after white bell flowers, two broad leaves), herb Paris (one berry on four leaves, dark blue-black) and young yew seedlings. None of them has a strawberry-shaped fruit with seeds on the outside. If it does not look and smell like a small strawberry, do not eat it.

## eat
Ripe fruit is eaten raw or added to other food. There is rarely enough for jam. Young leaves can be dried for tea.

In parts of Central Europe the fox tapeworm is present, and some people cook or wash ground-level berries for that reason. The risk from berries is thought to be low but not zero; washing reduces it, cooking removes it, normal freezing does not.

## harvest
Fruit from about June to August, sometimes later in the mountains. Pick when fully red and soft. Leaves for tea in spring and early summer. Avoid path edges used by dogs, roadsides and sprayed areas.
