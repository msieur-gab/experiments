Tap water can stop when the power or the network fails. The German civil protection office (BBK) says an adult needs at least 1.5 litres to drink and 0.5 litres for cooking each day, so 2 litres a day, which is 20 litres for 10 days. For washing too, plan on 3 to 5 litres a day.

## how
1. Work out how much you need: 2 litres per adult per day for drinking and cooking. Store more for pregnant women, sick people, pets, and in hot weather.
2. Aim for 10 days. If that is too much at first, start with 3 days and build up.
3. Unopened bottled water from a shop is the safest stock. Note its best-before date.
4. If you fill your own containers, use food-grade containers with a tight lid, made of unbreakable plastic, ideally with a narrow neck.
5. To clean a container: wash it with soap and rinse. Mix 1 teaspoon of unscented household bleach into 1 quart (about 1 litre) of water, put it in, close the lid and shake so it touches every surface. Wait at least 30 seconds, pour it out, and let the container air-dry.
6. Fill with clean tap water, close tightly, and write "drinking water" and the date on it.
7. Keep it in a cool, dark place, away from petrol, paint and garden chemicals.
8. Replace water you filled yourself every 6 months. Use the old water for plants or washing.

## safety
Never use containers that held bleach, pesticides, fuel or other chemicals. Take water out with a clean cup or scoop, never with your hands. If you freeze water in plastic bottles, do not fill them to the top or they crack. If you are unsure whether stored water is still good, boil it before drinking (see water-boil).
