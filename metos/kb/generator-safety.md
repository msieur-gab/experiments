Generators, grills and camping stoves make carbon monoxide, an invisible gas with no smell. Used inside, they can kill a whole household in their sleep. Run them outside only, well away from the house.

## how
1. Put the generator outdoors, more than 20 feet (about 6 metres) from any window, door or air vent.
2. Use an extension cable long enough to reach that distance.
3. Have a battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm working whenever a generator runs, and one near every sleeping area.
4. Cook on a camping stove, charcoal grill or gas grill outside only.
5. Keep air vents and flues clear of snow, leaves and debris.
6. Test the alarm batteries twice a year, when the clocks change, and replace the alarm every 5 years or as the maker says.

## safety
Never run a generator inside the home, in a cellar or in a garage, even with the doors and windows open. Never heat the home with a gas cooker or oven. Never leave a car running in a garage. Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain or confusion near a generator or stove can mean carbon monoxide poisoning: get everyone into fresh air at once and call 112. Sleeping people and people who have been drinking can die before they notice anything. Do not siphon fuel with your mouth.
