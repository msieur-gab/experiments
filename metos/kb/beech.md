One of the most common forest trees in Central Europe. Its small triangular nuts are oily and nourishing once roasted, and its young spring leaves are edible. It grows in woodland on most soils except very wet ones, from lowlands up into the mountains.

## identify
- Bark smooth and silver-grey, even on large old trees.
- Nuts small, three-sided and shiny brown, usually two together inside a woody husk covered in soft bristles; the husk splits into four lobes when ripe.
- Leaves oval with a pointed tip, wavy edge with no real teeth, and straight parallel side veins; young leaves have fine silky hairs along the edge.
- Buds long, thin and pointed like a cigar, brown.
- Dead copper-brown leaves often stay on young trees and hedges through winter.
- Nuts fall September to October. Some years almost none, other years very many (mast years).

## lookalikes
- Hornbeam also has smooth grey bark, but the trunk looks twisted and muscled. Its leaves are sharply double-toothed and its fruit is a small nutlet on a three-lobed leafy wing.
- Sweet chestnut husks are densely covered in long, sharp spines, and the nuts are larger and rounded. See sweet-chestnut.
- Copper beech, with dark purple leaves, is the same species; its nuts are the same.
- No common toxic plant in Central Europe has nuts like beech.

## eat
- Peel off the thin brown shell with a thumbnail or knife. Many nuts are empty; keep the full ones.
- Roast the kernels in a dry pan for a few minutes until they smell nutty. Roasting is thought to reduce the substances that upset the stomach.
- Raw: a few at most. Children and anyone eating them for the first time should start with very few.
- Roasted kernels can be ground and added to flour or porridge. They were also pressed for oil in times of shortage.
- Very young leaves in spring, bright green and almost translucent, can be eaten raw in small amounts in salad. They turn tough within a few weeks.

## harvest
- Nuts: September to October, from the ground soon after they fall, before animals and mould get them. Spreading a cloth and shaking low branches helps.
- Leaves: April to early May, while still soft.
- Avoid nuts that are dark, mouldy or soft. Gather away from roads and heavily used paths.
