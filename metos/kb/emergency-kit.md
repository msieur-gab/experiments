The German civil protection office (BBK) advises a home supply for 10 days. Build it slowly: buy one or two extra packs each time you shop, use the oldest first, and replace what you use.

## how
1. Water: 2 litres per adult per day (see water-storage). Some of it bottled water; juice and other drinks count too.
2. Food that keeps without a fridge and can be eaten cold or cooked quickly: tinned fish, meat, vegetables and fruit, dried fruit, nuts, crispbread or rusks, cereal bars. Add baby food, special diets and pet food if you need them.
3. A way to cook without power: a camping stove with fuel, used outdoors (see generator-safety).
4. Light: a torch and a head torch with spare batteries. Matches or a lighter.
5. A radio that works without mains power: batteries, solar or hand crank, with spare batteries.
6. Phone power: charged power banks or spare batteries, a car charger.
7. Medicines: your own prescriptions (many people can get a month's supply), a first aid kit.
8. Hygiene: toilet paper, soap, wet wipes, hand sanitiser, nappies and sanitary products.
9. Warmth: blankets, sleeping bags, warm clothes (see heat-one-room).
10. Cash in small notes. Cash machines and card payment stop in a power cut.
11. A document folder with copies of ID, health card, vaccination card and other important papers, and a paper list of phone numbers: family, neighbours, doctor, municipality, energy company.
12. A grab bag in case you must leave: medicines, first aid, radio, torch, documents, cash, clothes for a few days with rain gear, food for 2 days in dust-proof packing, a water bottle.

## safety
Check dates once a year on a fixed day, use food that is about to expire, and replace it. Keep fuel and stoves away from the living space. Candles are a fire risk; prefer torches.
