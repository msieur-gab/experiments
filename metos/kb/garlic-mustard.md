A common hedge and woodland-edge plant that smells of garlic when crushed and tastes of garlic and mustard. It is a useful seasoning green in spring. It grows in half-shade along hedges, paths, woodland edges and gardens.

## identify
- Crushed leaves smell clearly of garlic.
- Leaves are heart-shaped to kidney-shaped at the base of the plant, with round teeth along the edge, on long stalks.
- Leaves higher on the stem are more triangular and pointed, and grow one at a time up the stem.
- Small white flowers with four petals in a cross, in clusters at the top, from April to June.
- After flowering: long, thin, upright seed pods, 3 to 6 cm, full of small black seeds.
- First year: a low rosette of rounded leaves. Second year: an upright, mostly unbranched stem 30 to 100 cm tall.
- Grows along hedges, woodland edges, shady paths, walls and gardens, often in groups.

## lookalikes
Without the garlic smell, the rounded leaves can look like violets (Viola), ground ivy (Glechoma) or young nettles. Violets have no garlic smell; nettles sting. None of these is dangerous.

The rosette leaves can look a little like lesser celandine (Ficaria verna), which is poisonous raw. Lesser celandine has glossy, hairless, dark green heart-shaped leaves, yellow star-shaped flowers, and no garlic smell.

Garlic mustard is not the same as wild garlic (ramsons). Wild garlic has long, broad, lance-shaped leaves and is the plant confused with deadly lily of the valley and autumn crocus.

If the plant has no garlic smell, do not treat it as garlic mustard.

## eat
- Young leaves raw in salads, sandwiches, or chopped into butter, cheese or sauces.
- Cooked, the flavour becomes milder. Add it at the end of cooking.
- Flowers and young seed pods can be eaten raw.
- Seeds can be used like mustard seed.
- Older leaves become bitter.
- Use it as a seasoning and side green, a handful at a time, not as a main vegetable every day.

## harvest
- Leaves from March to May, best before flowering. The first-year rosettes can also be picked in autumn and mild winters.
- Flowers in April and May; seeds from July to August when the pods turn brown.
- Pick a few leaves from each plant.
- Do not pick along roadsides, sprayed field edges or dog-walking paths. It often grows on exactly these edges.
