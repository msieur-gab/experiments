Hawthorn is a thorny shrub or small tree, one of the most common hedge plants in Central Europe. Its dark red berries (haws) ripen in autumn and stay into winter, and its young leaves are edible in spring.

## identify
- Shrub or small tree up to about 8 m, very dense.
- Straight, sharp thorns, 1 to 2 cm, on the twigs.
- Leaves small, deeply cut into three to seven lobes, shiny on top.
- Clusters of white (sometimes pink) five-petalled flowers in May, with a strong, heavy smell.
- Berries in clusters, dark red, about 8 to 12 mm, slightly oval, with a small dark ring of dried flower parts at the tip.
- Flesh pale yellow, dry and mealy, around one large stone (sometimes two or three).
- Grows in hedges, woodland edges, pasture, scrub.

## lookalikes
Guelder rose has three-lobed leaves like a small maple, no thorns, and shiny, translucent red berries in flat clusters with a flat seed. Its berries are poisonous raw.

Cotoneaster has red berries but no thorns and small, simple, unlobed leaves; the berries are mildly toxic. Firethorn has thorns and orange or red berries, but small unlobed evergreen leaves; it is a garden shrub and best avoided. Yew, bryony, spindle and holly also have red fruit in autumn; none has lobed leaves with thorns and mealy flesh around a stone.

Rowan has bright orange-red berries in large clusters and long leaves made of many leaflets; it must be cooked. Rose hips are larger, oval and full of hairy seeds.

## eat
The flesh of ripe haws can be eaten raw in small amounts, but it is bland and dry; spit out the stones. Better cooked into jelly (often with apples), sauce, syrup or fruit leather; strain out the stones. Dried haws can be ground into a meal after removing the stones.

Young leaves and leaf buds in spring are edible raw, in salad or as a snack. Flowers and leaves are also dried for tea.

Hawthorn is used in herbal medicine for the heart and circulation. People taking heart or blood pressure medicine should not eat large amounts.

## harvest
Young leaves in April and May; flowers in May; berries from about September to November, when deep red and slightly soft. Wear gloves for the thorns. Avoid roadsides and sprayed hedges along fields.
