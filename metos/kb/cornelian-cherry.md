title: Cornelian cherry
aka: cornel, cornelian cherry dogwood, European cornel, Kornelkirsche, Dirndl, Herlitze, Gelber Hartriegel, cornouiller mâle, cornouille, Cornus mas
tags: plant wild food tree
warn: Check the fruit is oblong like an olive, with one long stone. Red berries of yew, mezereon and honeysuckle grow in similar places and are poisonous.

A shrub or small tree that flowers yellow on bare branches at the very end of winter. In late summer it bears shiny red, olive-shaped fruit that are very sour, but good raw when fully ripe and excellent for jam and juice. It grows wild on warm, dry slopes and forest edges, often on limestone, and is planted in parks, hedges and gardens.

## identify
- Fruit shiny red to dark red, oblong like a small olive, about 1.5 to 2 cm long, hanging singly or in small groups on short stalks. Each has one hard, elongated stone.
- Flowers very early, February to March, before the leaves: small clusters of tiny yellow flowers all along the bare twigs, giving a hazy yellow look.
- Leaves in opposite pairs, oval, with smooth edges and side veins that curve forward towards the tip.
- Gently tear a leaf across: fine white threads stretch between the two halves.
- Bark grey-brown, flaking in small scales on older stems.
- Fruit ripen August to September.

## lookalikes
- Yew (Eibe, if) has red, cup-shaped fruits open at one end, showing a dark seed, and flat dark needles. Seeds and needles are deadly.
- Mezereon (Seidelbast, bois-joli) flowers pink-purple on bare twigs in late winter, and in summer has round, bright red berries packed directly along the stem. It is very poisonous.
- Fly honeysuckle (Rote Heckenkirsche) has small, round, red berries joined in pairs. They are poisonous.
- Guelder rose has clusters of round, translucent red berries. Toxic raw.
- Red dogwood (Roter Hartriegel), a relative, has dark blue-black berries in flat clusters. Not edible.
- Forsythia also flowers yellow early on bare branches, but its flowers are larger bells, and it bears no red fruit.
- Cornelian cherry is the only one with oblong, olive-shaped red fruit holding a single long stone, on a plant with opposite, smooth-edged leaves.

## eat
- Eat raw only when fully ripe: dark red, soft, and coming off easily or already fallen. Unripe fruit are hard and very astringent.
- Spit out the stone.
- Cook into jam, jelly, syrup or juice. Strain or sieve out the stones after simmering.
- Fruit can be dried, or salted while still firm like olives.
- Raw, eat a handful at a time; the sourness limits how much you want anyway.

## harvest
- August to September, sometimes into October. Shake the branches over a cloth; ripe fruit drop.
- Slightly underripe fruit picked off the shrub will soften if left a few days at room temperature.
- Planted shrubs in parks and hedges are often easy to find; avoid those beside busy roads.
