The bilberry is a low shrub of acid-soil forests, heaths and mountains, often covering the ground in large patches. Its small blue-black berries are sweet and nutritious, and the plant is common across the uplands and mountains of Central Europe.

## identify
- Low, bushy shrub, usually 20 to 50 cm high, often forming a carpet.
- Green, sharply angled (edged) twigs, even in winter.
- Small oval leaves, finely toothed, light green, falling in autumn.
- Single round berries, 5 to 8 mm, blue-black, often with a light bloom, hanging under the leaves.
- A small flat round crown (ring) at the tip of each berry.
- Flesh and juice are dark purple and stain fingers and mouth.
- Grows in conifer and mixed forests, heaths and mountain slopes on acid soil.

## lookalikes
Bog bilberry (bog whortleberry) grows in moors and mountains. It has blue-grey, untoothed leaves, round brown twigs and pale flesh inside the berry. Large amounts have been reported to cause nausea and dizziness; do not collect it as bilberry.

Herb Paris grows in damp woods: a single stem with four broad leaves in a cross and one blue-black berry on top. It is poisonous. Deadly nightshade is a tall soft plant, up to 1.5 m, with shiny black cherry-sized berries sitting on a green five-pointed star. It is very poisonous. Spurge laurel is a small evergreen shrub with black berries; it is poisonous.

Crowberry has black berries on a shrub with needle-like leaves. It is not dangerous but has little taste.

A bilberry is a small single berry on a low, many-leaved shrub, with a crown at the tip and purple flesh. If any of these do not match, do not eat it.

## eat
Ripe berries are good raw, or as jam, compote, juice or dried fruit. Dried bilberries keep well. They are traditionally eaten for mild diarrhoea, but this does not replace water and salts.

In parts of Central Europe the fox tapeworm is present, and some people cook or wash ground-level berries for that reason. The risk from berries is thought to be low but not zero; washing reduces it, cooking removes it, normal freezing does not.

## harvest
From about July to September, later at higher altitude. Pick by hand or with a berry comb, gently, so the shrub is not torn. Avoid patches next to roads and the edges of paths used by dogs. In some protected areas picking is restricted.
