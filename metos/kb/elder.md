The elder is a common shrub or small tree of hedges, woodland edges and villages. Its flowers in early summer and its black berries in late summer are both widely used, but the berries must be cooked.

## identify
- Woody shrub or small tree, up to about 10 m, often with several stems.
- Grey-brown bark, corky and furrowed on older wood, with small raised dots on young twigs.
- Young branches have a soft white pith inside.
- Leaves in opposite pairs, each made of five to seven toothed leaflets; unpleasant smell when crushed.
- Large flat heads of small creamy-white flowers in May and June, with a sweet smell.
- Ripe berries small, shiny, purple-black, in large flat heads that hang down under their weight, on red-purple stalks.
- Grows in hedges, woodland edges, near houses, on nitrogen-rich soil.

## lookalikes
Dwarf elder (danewort) is poisonous. It is not woody: it grows each year from the ground to 1 or 2 m and dies back in winter. Its flower heads have purple or reddish tips (anthers) and stay upright, and its black berries stay upright on the plant. It often grows in large patches and smells unpleasant. Only pick from a woody shrub or tree.

Red-berried elder has red berries in egg-shaped clusters and flowers earlier, in April and May. Its seeds are toxic; do not use it.

Dogwood has black berries in flat clusters, but leaves are untoothed with curved veins and stems are often red. Do not eat them. Guelder rose has white flat flower heads with large showy flowers around the edge, lobed leaves and red berries.

Flower heads of the carrot family (hemlock, cow parsley) are also white and flat, but they grow on soft, non-woody stems. Never collect white flower heads from a plant that is not woody.

## eat
Berries: remove all stalks, then cook thoroughly (bring to the boil and simmer) before eating. Use for juice, syrup, jam or soup. Cooking makes them safe for most people; raw berries can cause vomiting and diarrhoea. Do not use green or unripe berries.

Flowers: used for syrup, cordial, tea and fritters in batter. Shake out insects rather than washing, and remove the green stalks as much as possible.

Never use leaves, bark or stems as food.

## harvest
Flowers from May to June, on a dry sunny morning when freshly opened. Berries from August to September, when the whole cluster is dark and the stalks have turned red. Cut the whole cluster and strip berries with a fork. Avoid roadsides.
