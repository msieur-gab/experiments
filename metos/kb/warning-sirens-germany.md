The siren does not tell you what the danger is. It tells you to get inside and find out. The details come over radio, TV, warning apps and official websites.

## how
Two signals are used nationwide:
- Warning: a howling tone that rises and falls for one minute. Danger.
- All-clear: a steady howling tone for one minute. The danger is over.

When you hear the warning:
1. Go into the nearest building and stay there. If you are outside, follow police and fire brigade announcements.
2. Take in passers-by who are outside, and tell other people in the building.
3. Close windows and doors. Turn off fans and air conditioning, and close window vents.
4. Go to an inner room with few or no outside windows.
5. Turn on the radio (FM, regional station) or the TV, and check the NINA app. Follow what the authorities say.
6. In a car: close the windows, turn off the ventilation, keep the radio on, and drive to the nearest building unless told otherwise.
7. Stay inside and keep windows shut until the all-clear.

## safety
Only phone in a real emergency, so the lines stay free. Do not light candles in a sealed room, they use up air. Towns can use extra local siren signals; ask your town hall what they mean. Germany tests its warning systems on the national warning day (Warntag), usually the second Thursday in September, with a test warning around 11:00 and the all-clear around 11:45.
