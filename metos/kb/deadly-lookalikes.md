These plants cause most serious poisonings in Central Europe. They often grow next to edible plants and look similar.

## identify
Hemlock (Conium maculatum): tall carrot-family plant with fine, fern-like leaves, white umbrella-shaped flower heads, and a smooth hollow stem with purple blotches. Mousy smell when crushed.

Water hemlock (Cicuta): carrot-family plant of wet places. Among the most poisonous plants in Europe.

Autumn crocus (Colchicum): spring leaves look like wild garlic but have no garlic smell. Pink crocus-like flowers in autumn.

Lily of the valley (Convallaria): two broad leaves wrapped around a stem, bell-shaped white flowers. Toxic to the heart.

Foxglove (Digitalis): soft hairy leaves in a rosette in its first year, tall spikes of purple bell flowers later. Leaves are confused with comfrey. Toxic to the heart.

Death cap (Amanita phalloides): a mushroom responsible for most mushroom deaths. Never eat wild mushrooms unless identified by an expert, in person.

## safety
Avoid all wild plants of the carrot family (white umbrella flower heads) unless you are an expert. Their edible and deadly members look alike.

## symptoms
Vomiting, diarrhoea, racing or irregular heartbeat, confusion, trouble breathing. Symptoms from autumn crocus and death cap can start many hours later. Seek help immediately and keep a sample of the plant.
