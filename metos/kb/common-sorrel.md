A meadow plant with arrow-shaped leaves and a strong sour, lemony taste. It is a classic soup and sauce green. It grows in grassland, meadows and along field edges almost everywhere.

## identify
- Arrow-shaped leaves: two pointed lobes at the base point backwards and down, towards the stalk.
- A thin papery sheath wraps the stem where each leaf joins it.
- Sharp, sour, lemony taste when chewed.
- Lower leaves have long stalks; upper leaves have no stalk and clasp the stem.
- Leaves are soft, matt green, smooth, 5 to 15 cm long.
- Flowers in May to July: loose spikes of small reddish-brown flowers on an upright stem, 30 to 100 cm tall.
- Grows in meadows, pastures, lawns, field edges and road verges, in the open, not in deep shade.

## lookalikes
Lords-and-ladies (Arum maculatum) is poisonous. Eating even a bit causes immediate burning pain and swelling in the mouth and throat. Its arrow-shaped leaves are glossy, have a network of veins, often dark spots, and grow in shady woods and hedges. They have no papery sheath and do not taste sour. Never taste-test a leaf to tell them apart: check the sheath, the vein pattern and the habitat.

Bindweeds (Convolvulus, Calystegia) also have arrow-shaped leaves, but they climb and twine around other plants and have large white or pink trumpet flowers.

Sheep's sorrel (Rumex acetosella) is smaller, with lobes that spread sideways. It is also edible and also high in oxalic acid.

Young docks (other Rumex) have larger leaves without the arrow shape. They are not dangerous but are less pleasant.

## eat
- Young leaves raw in salads, in small amounts, for a lemony note.
- Cooked in soups, sauces (classic with fish and eggs) and omelettes. It turns olive-brown when cooked, which is normal.
- Cooking with milk, cream or cheese is a traditional way to take the edge off.
- Treat it as a seasoning or side green: a handful, not a big bowl, and not every day.
- Do not cook it in aluminium or iron pans, which react with the acid.

## harvest
- Leaves from March to June, before flowering; new leaves again in autumn if the plants are cut.
- Pick single young leaves and leave the plant.
- Do not pick from road verges, sprayed fields, grazed pastures with heavy manure, or dog-walking meadows.
