title: Wild garlic
aka: wild garlic, ramsons, ramson, bear garlic, baerlauch, allium ursinum, ail des ours
tags: plant wild food
warn: Deadly lookalikes. Lily of the valley and autumn crocus grow in the same places and have similar leaves. Three or four autumn crocus leaves can kill. If in doubt, do not eat it.
see: deadly-lookalikes, foraging-rules
source: German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) press releases on wild garlic; Austrian food safety agency (AGES)

A spring woodland plant with a strong garlic smell. Delicious, but every year people are poisoned, sometimes fatally, by confusing it with toxic plants.

## identify
- Each leaf grows on its own thin stalk straight out of the ground.
- Leaves are soft, bright green, matt underneath, with one clear central vein.
- A crushed leaf smells strongly of garlic.
- White star-shaped flowers in round clusters, from about May.
- Found in damp, shady deciduous woods and along streams, from about March to May.

Lily of the valley: usually two leaves wrapped around one stem, firm, shiny underneath, no garlic smell.
Autumn crocus: several leaves grow together from one point, rubbery, no garlic smell. Flowers only in autumn.

## safety
The smell test is unreliable once your hands already smell of garlic from earlier leaves. Check every single leaf, one by one. Never pull up handfuls. Wash wild leaves well.

## eat
Raw in salads, as pesto, in soups and with eggs or potatoes. Best before flowering.
