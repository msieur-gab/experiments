When the heating fails, keep the warmth in one small space. Gather everyone in one room, close it off, insulate it, and dress in layers. Bodies together keep a room warmer.

## how
1. Choose one small room for everyone and keep its door closed.
2. Hang blankets over the windows and lay rugs or blankets on the floor. Floors get cold first.
3. Seal gaps around windows and doors with tape, blankets or plastic, but keep a way out you can use quickly.
4. Dress in layers, with wool closest to the skin. Wear a hat, socks and mittens.
5. Sleep on a sleeping mat in a sleeping bag, all in the same room. A small den under a table covered with blankets holds warmth well.
6. Air the room for a few minutes regularly, even when it is cold, to let in fresh air.
7. If you have a wood stove, fireplace or tiled stove, use it, following its safety rules.
8. If the home stays too cold, go to friends, family or a public warming place.

## safety
Never use a gas cooker or oven to heat the home. Never bring a charcoal or gas grill, camping stove or generator inside. Do not leave stoves or candles burning while you sleep. Keep candles in a holder or a bowl of water, away from curtains. A carbon monoxide alarm adds safety with any fuel-burning heater. If someone may be dangerously cold (hypothermia), get medical help at once: call 112. Never thaw a frozen pipe with an open flame.
