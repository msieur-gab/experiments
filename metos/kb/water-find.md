Plan for about 4 litres per person per day for drinking and basic hygiene. More in heat or during hard work.

## how
Rain: spread a clean tarp, sheet or plastic over a frame or use a clean roof gutter. Discard the first rain from a dirty surface. Collect into clean, covered containers.

Springs: water coming out of the ground is often cleaner than surface water, but still treat it.

Streams and rivers: take water from fast-flowing parts, upstream of villages, farms, roads and animals.

Dew: in the early morning, wipe dew from grass or a clean surface with a cloth and wring it out.

At home: water heaters, toilet cisterns (the tank, not the bowl, and never if chemical cleaners were added) and pipes still hold water when supply stops.

## safety
Avoid stagnant water, water near dead animals, below fields where fertiliser or pesticides are used, near mines, factories or roads after floods. Fuel and chemicals cannot be removed by boiling or bleach.
