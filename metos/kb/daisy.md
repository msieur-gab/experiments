title: Daisy
aka: common daisy, lawn daisy, English daisy, bruisewort, Gänseblümchen, pâquerette, Bellis perennis

The small white-and-yellow flower of lawns and short grass. Flowers, buds and young leaves are edible, with a mild, slightly nutty or bitter taste. It flowers in almost every month and is one of the easiest wild plants to recognise.

## identify
- A single flower on each short, leafless, slightly hairy stalk, growing from the base.
- Flower 1.5 to 3 cm wide: white petals, often pink-tipped underneath, around a yellow centre.
- Flowers close at night and in rain.
- Leaves in a flat rosette at ground level, spoon-shaped, slightly hairy, with shallow round teeth.
- No leaves on the flower stalk.
- Low, 3 to 15 cm.
- Grows in lawns, meadows, parks and short grass, in sun.
- Flowers from about February to November, longer in mild winters.

## lookalikes
Oxeye daisy (Leucanthemum vulgare) has larger flowers on tall leafy stems, 30 to 80 cm, in meadows. It is not dangerous and is often eaten too, but has a stronger taste.

Mayweeds and chamomiles (Tripleurospermum, Matricaria, Anthemis) have similar white-and-yellow flowers, but on branched, leafy stems with fine feathery leaves. Not dangerous, but not daisies.

Fleabanes (Erigeron) have thin white or pale lilac petals on leafy stems. Not daisies.

No dangerous plant in Central Europe looks like a daisy with leafless flower stalks and a flat rosette of spoon-shaped leaves.

## eat
- Flowers and buds raw in salads, on bread or as decoration.
- Young leaves raw or cooked; older leaves are more bitter and better cooked in soups.
- Flower buds can be pickled in vinegar and salt like capers.
- Flowers can be dried for tea.
- It is mild and safe in normal food amounts. Start small if you are prone to allergies.

## harvest
- Flowers almost all year, best from March to October. Pick open flowers in the late morning, when dry.
- Young leaves in spring.
- Pick heads with your fingers. A lawn regrows quickly.
- Avoid lawns treated with weed killer or fertiliser, parks, roadsides, sports fields and dog-walking areas.
