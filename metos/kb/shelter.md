warn: The ground takes heat from your body faster than the air. Always insulate yourself from the ground.

In bad weather, shelter comes before water and food. Losing body heat can kill within hours, especially when wet and windy.

## how
Choosing a place:
- Out of the wind, on slightly raised, dry ground.
- Not in dry riverbeds or hollows that flood.
- Not under dead trees or loose branches.
- Close to water and firewood, but not right beside the water.

Debris hut:
1. Lean a long, strong branch from a stump or fork down to the ground, as a ridge pole just longer than your body.
2. Lean sticks along both sides to make a narrow tent shape, only a little bigger than you.
3. Pile dry leaves, grass and branches on top until it is at least an arm's length thick.
4. Fill the inside floor with a thick layer of dry leaves or branches to lie on.
5. Block the entrance with a pile of leaves once inside.

## safety
Stay dry: wet clothes lose most of their warmth. Change or dry clothes as soon as possible. A small shelter is warmer than a big one.

## symptoms
Hypothermia: shivering, clumsiness, slurred speech, confusion. Shivering can stop when it gets worse. Get the person dry, insulated from the ground, out of the wind, and warm them with body heat and warm drinks if they can swallow.
